# Force Majeure

## Traditional Version

> Neither party shall be liable for any delay or failure to perform its obligations under this Agreement (other than payment obligations) to the extent that such delay or failure is caused by circumstances beyond such party's reasonable control, including but not limited to acts of God, fire, flood, earthquake, hurricane, tornado, epidemic, pandemic, war, terrorism, civil unrest, strikes, lockouts, labor disputes, government actions, embargoes, sanctions, power failures, internet failures, telecommunications failures, or any other cause beyond the reasonable control of the affected party (each, a "Force Majeure Event"). The affected party shall promptly notify the other party of the Force Majeure Event and shall use commercially reasonable efforts to mitigate its effects. If a Force Majeure Event continues for more than ninety (90) consecutive days, either party may terminate this Agreement upon written notice to the other party.

## Plain Language Rewrite

> **1. Excused Performance**
>
> Neither party is liable for delays or failures in performing its obligations (except payment obligations) caused by events outside its reasonable control.
>
> **2. Examples of Force Majeure Events**
>
> Force majeure events include:
>
> 1. Natural disasters (earthquake, flood, hurricane, wildfire).
> 2. Public health emergencies (epidemic, pandemic).
> 3. War, terrorism, or civil unrest.
> 4. Government actions (embargoes, sanctions, orders).
> 5. Infrastructure failures (power grid, internet backbone, telecommunications) not caused by the affected party's negligence.
> 6. Labor strikes or lockouts not involving the affected party's own employees.
>
> **3. Obligations of the Affected Party**
>
> The party affected by a force majeure event will:
>
> 1. Notify the other party within 5 days after the event begins.
> 2. Describe the expected impact and estimated duration.
> 3. Take reasonable steps to minimize the impact on the other party.
> 4. Resume performance as soon as reasonably possible.
>
> **4. Termination Right**
>
> If a force majeure event prevents performance for more than 60 consecutive days, either party may terminate this Agreement by giving 15 days' written notice. If the affected party resumes performance during the notice period, the termination notice is cancelled.
>
> **5. What Force Majeure Does Not Excuse**
>
> Force majeure does not excuse:
>
> 1. Payment obligations.
> 2. Failures caused by the affected party's own negligence or lack of preparation.
> 3. Economic hardship, market changes, or increased costs.
> 4. Loss of key personnel.

## What Changed and Why

- **Added notification timeline.** The traditional version says "promptly" — the rewrite gives a concrete 5-day window.
- **Added content requirements for notification.** The affected party must describe impact and estimated duration, not just announce the event.
- **Shortened the termination trigger.** Reduced from 90 to 60 days. Three months of non-performance is often too long for the non-affected party to wait.
- **Added a cancellation mechanism.** If performance resumes during the notice period, termination is cancelled — preventing premature exits.
- **Listed what force majeure does NOT cover.** This is often more important than what it covers. Economic hardship and personnel loss are commonly (and incorrectly) claimed as force majeure.
- **Organized the examples.** Replaced the run-on list with categorized numbered items.
- **Added negligence carve-out for infrastructure failures.** A party that fails to maintain backup systems shouldn't invoke force majeure for predictable outages.
