# Limitation of Liability

## Traditional Version

> IN NO EVENT SHALL EITHER PARTY BE LIABLE TO THE OTHER PARTY FOR ANY INDIRECT, INCIDENTAL, CONSEQUENTIAL, SPECIAL, PUNITIVE, OR EXEMPLARY DAMAGES, INCLUDING BUT NOT LIMITED TO DAMAGES FOR LOSS OF PROFITS, GOODWILL, USE, DATA, OR OTHER INTANGIBLE LOSSES (EVEN IF SUCH PARTY HAS BEEN ADVISED OF THE POSSIBILITY OF SUCH DAMAGES), ARISING OUT OF OR IN CONNECTION WITH THIS AGREEMENT, WHETHER BASED ON WARRANTY, CONTRACT, TORT (INCLUDING NEGLIGENCE), STRICT LIABILITY, OR ANY OTHER LEGAL THEORY. EXCEPT FOR THE OBLIGATIONS SET FORTH IN SECTIONS 8 (INDEMNIFICATION) AND 9 (CONFIDENTIALITY), IN NO EVENT SHALL EITHER PARTY'S AGGREGATE LIABILITY ARISING OUT OF OR RELATED TO THIS AGREEMENT, WHETHER IN CONTRACT, TORT, OR OTHERWISE, EXCEED THE TOTAL AMOUNTS PAID OR PAYABLE BY CUSTOMER TO PROVIDER DURING THE TWELVE (12) MONTH PERIOD IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING THE EVENT GIVING RISE TO THE CLAIM.

## Plain Language Rewrite

> **1. No Indirect Damages**
>
> Neither party will be liable to the other for indirect, incidental, consequential, special, or punitive damages — including lost profits, lost data, or business interruption — regardless of the legal theory and even if the party knew those damages were possible.
>
> **2. Liability Cap**
>
> Each party's total liability under this Agreement will not exceed the fees Customer paid to Provider in the 12 months before the event that caused the claim.
>
> **3. Exceptions**
>
> The limits in Sections 1 and 2 do not apply to:
>
> 1. Either party's indemnification obligations (Section 8).
> 2. Either party's breach of confidentiality obligations (Section 9).
> 3. Either party's willful misconduct or fraud.
> 4. Customer's obligation to pay fees owed under this Agreement.
> 5. Provider's breach of data protection obligations (Section 10).

## What Changed and Why

- **Removed ALL-CAPS.** Courts in most U.S. jurisdictions no longer require all-caps for liability limitations to be enforceable. Clear headings and plain language serve the same conspicuousness function.
- **Simplified the damages list.** Replaced "including but not limited to" with a concise dash-separated list. "Including" is already non-exhaustive at law.
- **Made the cap concrete.** "Fees Customer paid in the 12 months before the event" is easier to calculate than "amounts paid or payable."
- **Listed carve-outs explicitly.** The traditional version referenced two sections. The rewrite adds willful misconduct, payment obligations, and data protection — common and important carve-outs that are often negotiated.
- **Removed legal theory enumeration.** "Whether based on warranty, contract, tort..." adds length without substance; the "regardless of the legal theory" phrasing covers all bases more efficiently.
