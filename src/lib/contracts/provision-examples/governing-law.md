# Governing Law and Dispute Resolution

## Traditional Version

> This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Delaware, without regard to its conflict of laws principles. Any dispute, claim, or controversy arising out of or relating to this Agreement or the breach, termination, enforcement, interpretation, or validity thereof, including the determination of the scope or applicability of this agreement to arbitrate, shall be determined by arbitration in Wilmington, Delaware before one (1) arbitrator. The arbitration shall be administered by JAMS pursuant to its Comprehensive Arbitration Rules and Procedures. Judgment on the Award may be entered in any court having jurisdiction. This clause shall not preclude the parties from seeking provisional remedies in aid of arbitration from a court of appropriate jurisdiction. Each party irrevocably submits to the exclusive jurisdiction of the state and federal courts located in New Castle County, Delaware for any litigation arising out of or relating to this Agreement that is not subject to arbitration. Each party irrevocably waives any objection to venue in such courts and any claim that such courts are an inconvenient forum.

## Plain Language Rewrite

> **1. Governing Law**
>
> This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of [State], without regard to conflict-of-laws rules.
>
> **2. Informal Resolution**
>
> Before starting formal dispute resolution, the parties will try to resolve disputes informally:
>
> 1. The party raising the dispute will send a written description of the issue to the other party.
> 2. Within 15 days, each party will designate a senior representative to discuss resolution.
> 3. The representatives will attempt to resolve the dispute within 30 days.
>
> **3. Formal Dispute Resolution**
>
> If the parties cannot resolve the dispute informally within 45 days, either party may:
>
> 1. **For disputes under $250,000**: Submit the dispute to binding arbitration administered by [arbitration body] under its then-current rules, before a single arbitrator, in [City, State].
> 2. **For disputes over $250,000**: File suit in the state or federal courts located in [County, State].
>
> **4. Court Jurisdiction**
>
> Each party consents to the exclusive jurisdiction of the courts in [County, State] for disputes subject to litigation.
>
> **5. Equitable Relief**
>
> Either party may seek injunctive or other equitable relief from any court of competent jurisdiction at any time, without first going through the informal resolution or arbitration process above. This includes relief to protect intellectual property rights or Confidential Information.
>
> **6. Fees**
>
> Each party will bear its own attorneys' fees and costs, unless a court or arbitrator orders otherwise.
>
> **7. Jury Waiver**
>
> Each party waives its right to a jury trial for any dispute arising under this Agreement.

## What Changed and Why

- **Added informal resolution.** Most contract disputes can be resolved through executive-level conversations without formal proceedings. The traditional version jumps straight to arbitration.
- **Tiered dispute resolution.** Small disputes go to faster, cheaper arbitration. Larger disputes go to court where discovery and appeals are available. The traditional version forces all disputes into arbitration regardless of size.
- **Bracketed the jurisdiction.** The traditional version hardcodes Delaware. The rewrite uses brackets to signal that governing law is a business decision that should be negotiated, not defaulted.
- **Added equitable relief carve-out.** Both parties need the ability to seek emergency injunctive relief (e.g., to stop IP theft or data breaches) without waiting for informal resolution to play out.
- **Added fee allocation.** The traditional version is silent on who pays legal fees — which defaults to each party bearing its own in most U.S. jurisdictions, but should be stated explicitly.
- **Separated jury waiver.** This is a significant right being waived and deserves its own visible section rather than being buried in boilerplate.
