# Confidentiality

## Traditional Version

> "Confidential Information" shall mean any and all information, whether written, oral, electronic, or otherwise, that is designated as confidential or that reasonably should be understood to be confidential given the nature of the information and the circumstances of disclosure, including but not limited to trade secrets, business plans, financial information, customer lists, technical data, product plans, research and development, source code, algorithms, know-how, processes, and inventions. The Receiving Party shall: (a) hold all Confidential Information of the Disclosing Party in strict confidence; (b) not disclose such Confidential Information to any third party without the prior written consent of the Disclosing Party, except to the Receiving Party's employees, contractors, and agents who have a need to know and who are bound by confidentiality obligations no less restrictive than those contained herein; and (c) not use such Confidential Information for any purpose other than as necessary to perform its obligations under this Agreement. The obligations set forth in this Section shall survive the expiration or termination of this Agreement for a period of five (5) years.

## Plain Language Rewrite

> **1. What Is Confidential**
>
> "Confidential Information" means any non-public information that a party shares with the other party under this Agreement and identifies as confidential — or that a reasonable person would consider confidential given its nature.
>
> **2. What Is Not Confidential**
>
> Confidential Information does not include information that:
>
> 1. Is or becomes publicly available through no fault of the Receiving Party.
> 2. The Receiving Party already knew before receiving it from the Disclosing Party.
> 3. The Receiving Party receives from a third party without confidentiality restrictions.
> 4. The Receiving Party independently develops without using Confidential Information.
>
> **3. Obligations**
>
> The Receiving Party will:
>
> 1. Protect Confidential Information using at least the same care it uses for its own confidential information (and no less than reasonable care).
> 2. Use Confidential Information only to perform its obligations under this Agreement.
> 3. Limit access to employees and contractors who need it and who are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as protective as this section.
>
> **4. Required Disclosures**
>
> The Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information if required by law, regulation, or court order — but only after:
>
> 1. Notifying the Disclosing Party promptly (if legally permitted).
> 2. Cooperating with the Disclosing Party's efforts to obtain a protective order.
> 3. Disclosing only the minimum amount required.
>
> **5. Duration**
>
> Confidentiality obligations last for 3 years after this Agreement ends. For trade secrets, obligations last as long as the information remains a trade secret.
>
> **6. Return of Information**
>
> When this Agreement ends, each party will return or destroy the other party's Confidential Information within 30 days, and will confirm destruction in writing on request.

## What Changed and Why

- **Added exclusions.** The traditional version defines what *is* confidential but omits the standard carve-outs (public information, prior knowledge, independent development). These exclusions are essential.
- **Added compelled disclosure.** The traditional version is silent on what happens when a court or regulator requires disclosure. The rewrite provides a practical process.
- **Shortened the survival period.** Five years is common but often longer than needed for most commercial information. Three years with a trade-secret carve-out better reflects the value decay of most business information.
- **Removed the laundry list.** "Trade secrets, business plans, financial information, customer lists..." — these illustrative lists add length without changing the legal meaning of "non-public information identified as confidential."
- **Added a return/destruction timeline.** Thirty days gives parties a practical window for compliance.
