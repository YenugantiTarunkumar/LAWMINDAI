# Provision Patterns: AI/ML Contract Terms

> Contract provisions specific to artificial intelligence and machine learning products and services. This is a rapidly evolving area — these patterns reflect current best practices but should be reviewed as laws and norms develop.

---

## 1. Training Data Usage Rights and Restrictions

### Traditional Version

> **Use of Customer Data for Model Training.** Provider shall not use, and hereby covenants that it will not use, Customer Data, including but not limited to any inputs, outputs, prompts, queries, or feedback provided by Customer or its authorized users in connection with the Service (collectively, "Customer Content"), for the purpose of training, fine-tuning, improving, or otherwise developing any machine learning model, artificial intelligence system, or algorithm, whether proprietary to Provider or licensed from a third party, without the prior express written consent of Customer.

### Improved Version

> **1. No Training on Customer Data**
>
> Provider will not use Customer Data to train, fine-tune, or improve any AI model — including Provider's own models and any third-party models — unless Customer gives prior written consent.
>
> **2. What "Customer Data" Includes**
>
> For this section, Customer Data includes:
>
> 1. Inputs and prompts Customer sends to the Service.
> 2. Outputs the Service generates for Customer.
> 3. Feedback Customer provides about the Service.
> 4. Any data Customer uploads, transmits, or makes available through the Service.
>
> **3. Aggregated and De-identified Data**
>
> Provider may use aggregated, de-identified data derived from Customer's use of the Service to improve the Service, provided that:
>
> 1. The data cannot reasonably be used to identify Customer or any individual.
> 2. Provider complies with applicable data protection laws when de-identifying data.
> 3. Provider documents its de-identification process and makes it available to Customer on request.
>
> **4. Opt-In for Training**
>
> If Customer wants to allow Provider to use Customer Data for model training, the parties will document the scope, purpose, and duration of that consent in a separate written agreement.

**What changed:** Removed legalistic "hereby covenants." Made the no-training commitment the headline. Enumerated what constitutes Customer Data (removing ambiguity about prompts and outputs). Added a practical path for opt-in consent. Addressed the aggregated/de-identified data carve-out that many AI providers need.

## 2. Model Ownership vs. Output Ownership

### Traditional Version

> **Intellectual Property in Models and Outputs.** As between the parties, Provider shall retain all right, title, and interest in and to the AI Models, including all underlying algorithms, neural network architectures, model weights, training methodologies, and related intellectual property. Customer shall own all right, title, and interest in the Outputs generated by the Service using Customer's Inputs, subject to Provider's underlying intellectual property rights in the AI Models. For the avoidance of doubt, nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to grant Customer any right, title, or interest in or to the AI Models or any component thereof. Provider hereby grants to Customer a perpetual, irrevocable, non-exclusive license to use, reproduce, modify, and distribute the Outputs for any lawful purpose.

### Improved Version

> **1. Provider Owns the Models**
>
> Provider owns the AI models that power the Service, including their algorithms, architectures, weights, and training methods. This Agreement does not transfer any model ownership to Customer.
>
> **2. Customer Owns the Outputs**
>
> Customer owns the outputs that the Service generates from Customer's inputs, subject to two conditions:
>
> 1. Provider retains its pre-existing intellectual property in the underlying models.
> 2. If the same or similar output could be generated for other customers using different inputs, that output is not exclusive to Customer.
>
> **3. Customer's Rights to Outputs**
>
> Customer may use, copy, modify, and distribute outputs for any lawful purpose. This right survives the end of this Agreement.
>
> **4. No Guarantee of IP Protection**
>
> Provider does not guarantee that outputs qualify for copyright protection or any other intellectual property rights under applicable law. The availability of IP protection for AI-generated content varies by jurisdiction and is evolving.

**What changed:** Added the non-exclusivity caveat (critical for generative AI where multiple users may get similar outputs). Added an IP protection disclaimer (reflecting the uncertain legal status of AI-generated content). Removed "for the avoidance of doubt." Simplified ownership language.

## 3. AI-Generated Content Liability

### Traditional Version

> **Disclaimer Regarding AI-Generated Content.** CUSTOMER ACKNOWLEDGES AND AGREES THAT THE SERVICE UTILIZES ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE AND MACHINE LEARNING TECHNOLOGIES THAT MAY PRODUCE RESULTS THAT ARE INACCURATE, INCOMPLETE, OR OTHERWISE NOT FIT FOR ANY PARTICULAR PURPOSE. PROVIDER SHALL NOT BE LIABLE FOR ANY DAMAGES ARISING FROM CUSTOMER'S RELIANCE ON OR USE OF ANY OUTPUT GENERATED BY THE SERVICE. CUSTOMER ASSUMES ALL RISK ASSOCIATED WITH THE USE OF AI-GENERATED CONTENT AND SHALL BE SOLELY RESPONSIBLE FOR VERIFYING THE ACCURACY, COMPLETENESS, AND APPROPRIATENESS OF ALL OUTPUTS BEFORE RELYING UPON OR DISTRIBUTING THE SAME.

### Improved Version

> **1. AI Outputs May Be Inaccurate**
>
> The Service uses AI technology that may produce outputs that are inaccurate, incomplete, biased, or inappropriate. Provider does not guarantee the accuracy, reliability, or fitness of any output for any particular purpose.
>
> **2. Customer's Responsibility**
>
> Customer is responsible for:
>
> 1. Reviewing all outputs before relying on them or sharing them.
> 2. Verifying factual claims in outputs using independent sources.
> 3. Ensuring that Customer's use of outputs complies with applicable law.
> 4. Not using outputs in high-risk contexts (e.g., medical, legal, or financial decisions) without qualified human review.
>
> **3. Provider's Responsibility**
>
> Provider is responsible for:
>
> 1. Using commercially reasonable efforts to improve output quality over time.
> 2. Notifying Customer of known material deficiencies in the AI models.
> 3. Implementing safeguards designed to reduce harmful, biased, or illegal outputs.
>
> **4. Shared Risk**
>
> Neither party will be liable for damages caused solely by the other party's failure to meet its responsibilities under this section.

**What changed:** Removed ALL-CAPS formatting. Critically, added *Provider responsibilities* — traditional versions push all AI risk onto the customer, but providers also have a duty to build safe systems. Created a shared-risk framework. Specified high-risk use restrictions. Acknowledged bias as a specific risk.

## 4. Accuracy Disclaimers and Limitations

### Traditional Version

> **No Warranty of Accuracy.** Provider makes no representation or warranty, express or implied, regarding the accuracy, completeness, or reliability of any Output. Customer acknowledges that AI-generated content may contain errors, hallucinations, fabricated information, or biased results, and that Provider shall have no obligation to verify or correct any Output.

### Improved Version

> **1. Known Limitations**
>
> AI-generated outputs have known limitations, including:
>
> 1. **Hallucinations**: The AI may generate plausible-sounding but factually incorrect information.
> 2. **Bias**: Outputs may reflect biases present in training data.
> 3. **Staleness**: The AI's knowledge has a training cutoff date and may not reflect current information.
> 4. **Context errors**: The AI may misinterpret ambiguous inputs.
>
> **2. Accuracy Disclaimer**
>
> Provider does not warrant that outputs will be accurate, complete, current, or free from bias.
>
> **3. Prohibited Uses**
>
> Customer will not use outputs as the sole basis for:
>
> 1. Medical diagnosis or treatment decisions.
> 2. Legal advice to third parties.
> 3. Financial trading or investment decisions.
> 4. Decisions that have a material legal effect on individuals (e.g., employment, credit, housing).
> 5. Safety-critical systems where errors could cause physical harm.
>
> **4. Feedback Loop**
>
> Customer may report inaccurate or harmful outputs to Provider at [contact method]. Provider will review reported issues and use them to improve the Service.

**What changed:** Named specific AI failure modes (hallucinations, bias, staleness) rather than using a vague blanket disclaimer. Added a prohibited-use list tied to high-risk contexts. Created a feedback mechanism. Replaced passive "Provider shall have no obligation" with an active commitment to improve.

## 5. Human Oversight Requirements

### Traditional Version

> **Human Review.** Customer shall implement and maintain appropriate human oversight mechanisms with respect to any use of AI-generated Outputs in connection with decisions that may have a significant impact on individuals, in accordance with applicable laws, rules, and regulations, including but not limited to the European Union Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689).

### Improved Version

> **1. When Human Review Is Required**
>
> Customer will ensure that a qualified human reviews AI outputs before they are used for:
>
> 1. Decisions that materially affect an individual's legal rights, employment, credit, insurance, housing, or education.
> 2. Content published under Customer's name or brand.
> 3. Any use classified as "high-risk" under the EU AI Act or similar legislation.
> 4. Any context where Customer's internal policies require human approval.
>
> **2. What Human Review Means**
>
> Human review requires that a person with appropriate expertise:
>
> 1. Understands the AI output and the context in which it will be used.
> 2. Has the authority and ability to override or reject the AI output.
> 3. Does not treat the AI output as presumptively correct.
>
> **3. Provider's Transparency Obligations**
>
> To support human oversight, Provider will:
>
> 1. Provide documentation explaining the AI model's capabilities and limitations.
> 2. Make available, on request, information about the general logic of the AI system.
> 3. Not design the Service in a way that discourages or prevents human override of AI outputs.

**What changed:** Specified *when* human review is required (not just "in accordance with applicable laws"). Defined what meaningful human review looks like. Added Provider transparency obligations to enable effective human oversight. Referenced the EU AI Act specifically.

## 6. Responsible AI Commitments

### Traditional Version

> **Responsible AI.** Provider represents and warrants that it has implemented and shall maintain throughout the Term responsible artificial intelligence practices, including but not limited to policies and procedures designed to address fairness, transparency, accountability, and safety in the development and deployment of AI systems.

### Improved Version

> **1. Provider's AI Ethics Commitments**
>
> Provider commits to the following responsible AI practices:
>
> 1. **Fairness**: Testing AI models for bias across protected characteristics and taking reasonable steps to mitigate identified biases.
> 2. **Transparency**: Publishing documentation about the AI model's intended uses, known limitations, and evaluation results.
> 3. **Safety**: Implementing content filters and safeguards designed to prevent the generation of harmful, illegal, or dangerous content.
> 4. **Privacy**: Minimizing the collection and retention of personal data used in AI training and inference.
> 5. **Accountability**: Maintaining logs sufficient to investigate AI-related incidents and designating a responsible AI contact within Provider's organization.
>
> **2. AI Impact Assessment**
>
> Before making material changes to the AI models used in the Service, Provider will:
>
> 1. Conduct an impact assessment evaluating the change's effect on accuracy, bias, and safety.
> 2. Notify Customer of material model changes at least 30 days in advance.
> 3. Provide updated documentation reflecting the changes.
>
> **3. Customer's Responsible Use**
>
> Customer will:
>
> 1. Use the Service in accordance with Provider's acceptable use policy.
> 2. Not use the Service to generate content that is illegal, harmful, or designed to deceive.
> 3. Not use the Service to discriminate against individuals based on protected characteristics.
> 4. Report any harmful or unexpected AI behavior to Provider promptly.
>
> **4. Regulatory Compliance**
>
> Each party will comply with AI-specific laws and regulations applicable to its role (Provider as the AI system provider; Customer as the deployer). If new regulations require changes to the Service or Customer's use of it, the parties will cooperate to achieve compliance within the regulatory timeline.

**What changed:** Replaced vague "responsible AI practices" with five specific, measurable commitments. Added a model change notification process. Created mutual obligations (not just provider commitments). Addressed regulatory compliance as a shared responsibility. Added concrete actions (bias testing, impact assessments, content filters) rather than just policy references.

## 7. AI Transparency and Disclosure

### Traditional Version

> **Disclosure of AI Use.** Customer acknowledges that the Service incorporates artificial intelligence technologies. Provider shall provide such information regarding its AI systems as may be required by applicable law.

### Improved Version

> **1. AI Disclosure**
>
> Provider will clearly disclose:
>
> 1. Which features of the Service use AI or machine learning.
> 2. Whether outputs are fully AI-generated, AI-assisted, or human-generated.
> 3. The general type of AI model used (e.g., large language model, image generation model).
>
> **2. Customer's Downstream Disclosure**
>
> If Customer distributes AI-generated outputs to third parties, Customer is responsible for disclosing the AI-generated nature of that content where required by law or industry norms.
>
> **3. Labeling**
>
> Provider will support labeling or metadata features that allow Customer to identify AI-generated content, where technically feasible.

**What changed:** Made disclosure requirements specific and actionable. Added Customer's downstream disclosure responsibility. Included a labeling/metadata commitment to support provenance tracking.

---

*AI/ML contract terms are evolving rapidly. These patterns reflect practices as of 2024–2025 and should be reviewed as the EU AI Act, U.S. state AI laws, and international frameworks mature.*
